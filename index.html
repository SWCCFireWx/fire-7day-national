To add a day
  1. Pick the trigger and the date.
  2. Type what was observed (for example “scattered dry storms, RH 12–18%”).
  3. Tick the PSAs where it happened (PSAs forecast with that trigger are marked).
  4. Click Add confirmed day.
To remove a mistake
  1. Find it under Recorded confirmed days.
  2. Click the × next to the PSA. It turns gray and crossed out (click again to undo).
  3. Wrong date? Remove it, then add it again with the right date.
Then save
  1. Click Save confirmed days.
  2. GitHub opens with everything filled in. Sign in (a free account) and click Submit new issue.
  3. Reload this page in about 5 minutes to see it.

What this page is

Every night at about 3 am Mountain time a GitHub Action saves the previous day's national 7-Day Significant Fire Potential (all PSAs, all seven days), split by GACC into data/<GACC>/forecasts.csv. It then checks each GACC's previous day against that GACC's own trigger criteria and saves the result to data/<GACC>/observed.csv.

Use the bar at the top to pick a GACC, or National to see every GACC together. Each GACC has its own High Risk trigger types (letter, name, color) and thresholds, shown on the Criteria tab. To request a change to a GACC's triggers or criteria, email Krista King at krista.king@usda.gov. Lightning, Recreation and other by-hand trigger days can be confirmed by anyone on the Confirm triggers tab. Triggers are either checked automatically from RAWS, or confirmed by hand (like Lightning).

Forecast categories

How observed High Risk is decided

Each RAWS station is checked on its own. A PSA meets a trigger when enough stations hit it, not the PSA average.

  • Wind (W): an hour with sustained wind at or above the limit and RH at or below the limit, or several hours of gusts at or above the gust limit with low RH.
  • Burn Environment (B): afternoon max temperature well above the typical peak-season max with low RH. Typical peak-season max is estimated from station elevation unless a PSA value is set.
  • Lightning (L), Recreation (R) and other by-hand triggers: confirmed on the Confirm triggers tab. The Weather check tab lists Lightning forecasts waiting for review with the number of dry stations as context.

Changing the criteria

Trigger and criteria changes are made by the site owner. To request one, email Krista King at krista.king@usda.gov with the GACC and the wording or thresholds. The owner uses Site owner tools on the Criteria tab, where you can keep several named sets (for example “Standard” and “Hot season”) and switch which one is in use, starting on any date you choose. Each day is checked with the set that was in use that day, so earlier results keep their original criteria unless you edit that set.

Saving opens GitHub with the changes filled in; click Submit new issue there. An automatic step checks who sent it, checks the numbers, saves them, re-checks past days, and replies on that issue. Criteria changes from anyone other than the owner are not accepted. The site updates within a few minutes.

The Little/Low/Moderate risk levels depend on fuels (ERC and other indices), so the weather check covers the High Risk triggers only.

Confirming Lightning, Recreation and other by-hand days

Anyone can do this; no email needed. On the Confirm triggers tab (pick the GACC first), pick the trigger and date, describe the observed conditions, tick the PSAs that hit (PSAs forecast with that trigger are marked), and click Add confirmed day. Then click Save confirmed days, sign in to GitHub (a free account) and click Submit new issue. Click × on a recorded PSA to remove it. Once saved, the day counts as observed in the map, charts, verification and exports.

Weather data

The Weather data tab lists, for the GACC you pick, every RAWS used for verification (all RAWS inside its PSAs), which PSA each belongs to, how many days each reported, the typical peak-season max it is checked against, its latest ERC and how often it met each automatic trigger. Click a station for its daily values (about the last 120 days are kept here). It also shows what data each trigger relies on.

Map and Analysis

The Map tab shows the PSAs colored by the forecast as issued, by outcome (hit, false alarm, miss, correct quiet), or by season verification stats such as skill or bias. Step through days or press Play; click a PSA to open its verification. The Analysis tab shows reliability by observed ERC, minimum RH or peak gust; a calendar of outcomes by PSA; how often forecasts changed between Day 7 and Day 1; and weekly skill and bias.

Exporting

The Export tab builds an Excel workbook, a PDF report, or a plain data file (CSV) for any date range and any set of PSAs. Choose what to include: verification summary, verification by month, misses and false alarms to review, the color-coded forecast grid, forecast evolution, observed weather, confirmed by-hand trigger days, fires and the criteria used. For a PDF, choose “Save as PDF” as the destination in the print window.

Verification stats

  • POD: share of event days that were forecast “yes”. Higher is better.
  • FAR: share of “yes” forecasts with no event. Lower is better.
  • CSI: hits ÷ (hits + misses + false alarms). Higher is better.
  • Bias: “yes” forecasts ÷ event days. Above 1 means over-forecasting.
